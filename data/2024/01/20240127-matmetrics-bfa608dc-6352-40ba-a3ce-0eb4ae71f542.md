# 2024-01-27 - Judo Session: Shiai

## Techniques Practiced
- O\-uchi\-gari

## Session Description

My first judo competition, the Midlands Open at the University of Wolverhampton in Walsall, is now behind me\. It was a useful experience in some ways, but overall, it didn't go well\. I competed in the under\-90 kg category, which ended up having just three competitors\.My first match was against Mr Cucumber, a tall, strong judoka with a long reach who often attacked with a dropping sumi\-gaeshi\. I managed to defend against his sumi\-gaeshi attempts reasonably well, but I struggled to convert any of those defenses into meaningful groundwork\. Eventually, he threw me with a technique I can't quite recall, and I hesitated, unsure who had scored\. In that brief lapse of focus, he transitioned straight into a hold\-down and secured the win\. Dealing with someone with such a significant reach advantage was tough enough, but that momentary confusion definitely cost me\.My second match was against Mr Tomato, a slower but experienced left\-handed judoka—my first time facing a left\-hander in competition\. I managed to neutralize some of his early grips and attacks, but I couldn’t really impose my own judo or create a clear opening\. Eventually, he secured a strong gripping position and threw me cleanly with o\-soto\-gari for ippon\. Given his experience and my unfamiliarity with left\-handed fighters at the time, I don’t think I could have done much better in that particular match\.

## Notes

The structure of the day was challenging\. I arrived at the venue around 13:00, but didn’t step onto the mat until nearly 19:00\. Trying to stay warm, relaxed, and mentally switched on for six hours was far from ideal\. That alone was a learning experience\. It quickly became clear that I should avoid school\-style competitions in future and focus instead on senior or masters events with tighter scheduling\.

Overall, the day was disappointing—not just because I lost both matches, but because of the long waiting time and the sense that I wasn’t able to express my judo properly\. Still, it provided some early clarity\. I need to get my weight down to under 81 kg, and I need to be more selective about the competitions I enter\. This was only the beginning, but it set some important direction for what came next\.
