# 2024-03-23 - Judo Session: Shiai

## Techniques Practiced
- O\-uchi\-gari

## Session Description

Today's session involved a series of matches\. My first match ended quickly when my opponent unfortunately had to withdraw due to what seemed like a rib injury, resulting in an easy win for me\. The second match was a real grind, lasting over four minutes and going into golden score before I managed to clinch a victory with a half point\. In my third match, I secured a full point after more than two minutes by countering an attack with a well\-timed o\-uchi gari\.

Throughout these initial three matches, I felt I maintained superior grips, which effectively prevented my opponents from executing their techniques cleanly\. However, my fourth and final match presented a completely different challenge\. My opponent matched my grip fighting skills and appeared to have better overall judo\. I attempted to bait him with the same o\-uchi gari technique I had used earlier, but he countered flawlessly with a tsubame gaeshi, sweeping me onto my back for a full point\.

While I'm not sure if I could have won that final match, it was a clear learning experience\. It showed me that openly baiting an opponent simply doesn't work if they are fresh and technically sound\. This reinforces my belief in the fundamental importance of maintaining strong grip fighting, taking my time, and consistently applying positive judo\.

## Notes

Today was my second competition at 1st kyu \(brown belt\), held at the Samurai Judo Club in Kidderminster\. Overall, it was a successful endeavor\. Leading up to the competition, I had no injuries and maintained good weight and fitness, making my target of being under 81 kg a reality\.

In the tournament, I won my first three rounds and lost in the final, which secured me second place and a silver medal\. In hindsight, this event was an excellent opportunity to solidify my general approach in judo fights\. My current strategy centers on outlasting my opponents—using stamina to wear them down until they become tired and sloppy, which opens up scoring opportunities\. Additionally, my ground fighting has improved, making it less likely for me to lose on the floor\.

At the end of the day, coming away with 10 points, no injuries, and a silver medal is a success\. Each match provided valuable insights into my strengths and areas for improvement, and I’m motivated to keep refining my approach for future competitions\.
