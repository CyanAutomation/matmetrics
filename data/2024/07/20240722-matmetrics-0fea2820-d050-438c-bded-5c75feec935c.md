# 2024-07-22 - Judo Session: Randori

## Techniques Practiced
- Uchi\-mata
- O\-soto\-gari
- Ashi\-guruma
- Ko\-uchi\-gari

## Session Description

The session began with the usual warmup, followed by ground fighting randori, which I only partially joined on the second day\. After that, we moved into a short tachiwaza warmup with partners, doing uchikomi and nagekomi, before dedicating 45 minutes to stand\-up randori\. The group was generally strong, mainly composed of older Cadet and younger Senior judoka, with most being black belts, alongside a few brown, green, and blue belts\. On Sunday, I had the opportunity to partner with a couple of Team GB Masters black belts, which was a good test\. I actually felt like I did a little better than I expected against them; obviously, I was outmatched, but I managed to provide some solid resistance\. I even threw one of them with an Uchi\-mata, and almost got the other with an O\-soto\-gari\. The feedback I received for my O\-soto\-gari was to make sure I keep everything tight\. Looking at specific techniques, I only managed to make Ashi guruma work once, and that was as a counter on a lighter opponent; it failed pretty much every other time I tried it\. Ko\-uchi\-gari worked once too, which makes me think I should try to implement that more often in my randori\. My Uchi\-mata attempts worked about 40% of the time, so while it had some success, I definitely need to improve its overall effectiveness\.

## Notes

I have just concluded two days of training at the University of Bath\. Overall, there were four sessions, two on Sunday and two on Monday\. Although I couldn’t attend all of them, I managed to participate in most of each morning session\. Even then, it was thoroughly exhausting\.

Reflecting on the sessions, the main technical element I focused on was gripping and grip breaking\. It’s clear that a strong and dominant grip is crucial in Judo\. I established that I am comfortable with breaking off the sleeve hand using a gravity force push motion, but I need to think about my next move after that\.

However, it’s evident that Judo revolves around the lapel hand\. On a positive note, I learned a good grip break for my opponent’s hand on my lapel: the shoulder bump, which seemed to work 50% of the time once I started practicing it\. Less positively, my opponents could still break my posture and collapse the space\. I need to develop methods to prevent this or at least think about attacks and counters from this non\-dominant position\.

My hands are raw, and my fingers are sore, but overall it was a mostly positive experience\. The travel was hard, and I’m thankful to my family for supporting me in taking the time to spend six hours traveling and three hours training to attend this 2\-day training camp\.
