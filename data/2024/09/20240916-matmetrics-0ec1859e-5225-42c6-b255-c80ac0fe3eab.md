# 2024-09-16 - Judo Session: Shiai

## Techniques Practiced
- Uchi\-mata

## Session Description

My first opponent was Mr\. Coriander, a younger man, slightly taller than me\. As the match started, he came in aggressively with grips, which I managed to somewhat defend\. Every time he got a partial grip, he immediately went for a drop technique, which I was able to defend against\. After each failed attempt, he went into a tight turtle position, preventing me from transitioning to ne\-waza\. In hindsight, I should have worked harder to make that transition\. The match dragged on, with both of us getting more tired\. His strong grips prevented me from implementing many techniques, and soon fatigue and tightness reached their peak\. I remember feeling more exhausted than I’ve ever been in a match, just wishing for it to end\. Eventually, he attempted what I think was a tai otoshi, and due to sheer fatigue, I lost the will to fight and went over onto my back, losing the match\. My second match, not long after the first, was against Mr\. Ginger—a strong, tall young man with a back patch\. He had good grip\-fighting skills and consistently tried to nullify my lapel grip\. He didn’t seem to mind going for a double sleeve grip, which I’m becoming more confident in defending against, but I think he won the gripping game in this match\. His strategy was clear \- he aggressively attacked for grips, and the moment he secured one, he went straight for a technique\. This left me with very few openings for my own attacks, and I ended up with two penalties for passivity, even though I was trying my best to put in throws\. He favored uchi mata, and while I managed to defend against it, the repeated attempts took a toll on my energy\. At one point, I went in for an uchi mata, but he countered by lifting me in a jigotai stance and dropping me\. I avoided being thrown onto my back, but it earned him a half point\. We continued in a fatigued stalemate until the clock ran out, and he won due to the earlier half point\.

## Notes

I’ve just completed the day at the High Wycombe Kyu Grade Cup, and I’m feeling sore and bruised—especially my ego\. Although I was hoping to gain some points, I didn’t really need them, as I already have enough for my black belt\. My mindset going in was to practice gripping strategies and gain some more competition experience\.

Health\-wise, I was in good shape—80 kg, with no illness or injuries\. I arrived at 9 o’clock for weigh\-in, but my first match wasn’t until noon\. It was a full and busy day, with many competitors of different grades, shapes, and weights\. Unfortunately, my \-81 kg category was the last to get called\.

It was a frustrating day\. I had hoped to win at least one match and possibly take home a bronze medal\. The level of competition was better than I anticipated\. Even though I came in intending to practice gripping, it’s clear that some of my opponents had far superior gripping skills\. I definitely need to think about switching to Shohei Ono’s right\-hand grip when my high collar is taken, as well as being more proactive with early attacks to unsettle my opponents\.

That’s the key takeaway from today: if I don’t make my opponent uncomfortable, it gives them “clear air” to attack\. Moving forward, I need to focus on attacking earlier and more often to break their rhythm\.
